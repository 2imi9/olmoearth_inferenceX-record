\section{The problem}
\label{sec:intro}

A classification map from an Earth-observation model is usually applied where no reference labels exist. The model's
own confidence can mislead. Ai2's fine-tuned AWF model, as I ran it, was 0.93 accurate on the 299 of its 344
validation points where its confidence was 0.9 or more (mean 0.99) \claim{accuracy-needs-coverage}. A user with such a
map and few or no labels has five questions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Which windows should be reviewed first, when only a fraction can be checked (Section~\ref{sec:ranking})?
\item How do two maps of one area differ (Section~\ref{sec:compare})?
\item Once some windows are labelled, how wrong is the map, and which part of it can be used with a stated error
bound (Sections~\ref{sec:estimate} and~\ref{sec:zone})?
\item Which of two maps is more accurate, from as few labels as possible (Section~\ref{sec:whichmap})?
\item What does an agency's published stratified reference sample say about a map, with intervals that hold at its
sample size (Section~\ref{sec:agency})?
\end{enumerate}

\pkg{} answers them from the model's class scores. It pools the scores to windows of $4\times4$ pixels. Each window
carries a class and a confidence, the margin between the two highest scores. The package reads arrays and rasters and
loads no model, so it applies to any model that writes class scores (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}). In my evaluation,
labels grade the label-free readings and never train them \claim{labels-grade-never-train}.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{One tool} that answers the five questions for any classification map, after the fact. It refuses inputs
that would void its intervals, such as a review set offered as a random sample.
\item \textbf{A stratified betting interval valid at any sample size.} Its bets give every stratum the same slope, so
the interval needs no search over how the tested value splits among the strata (Section~\ref{sec:agency}).
\item \textbf{A test of confidence as the review signal}, across encoders, tasks, published products, Ai2's fine-tuned
models and another organisation's field map, against controls that do not use the model. It includes where the signal
fails.
\item \textbf{A public record.} Predictions were preregistered, and every number in this report traces to a claim
ledger checked against committed outputs. Failed predictions are reported as failed.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Related work.} Apart from the bet of the second contribution, the components come from earlier work.
Risk-coverage evaluation comes from selective classification \citep{geifman2017}, and softmax confidence is the
standard error-detection baseline \citep{hendrycks2017}. The estimators follow design-based map-accuracy practice
\citep{olofsson2014,stehman2019}. Prediction-powered inference \citep{angelopoulos2023ppi} addresses the same
estimation task. The agency intervals build on betting intervals for sampling without replacement
\citep{waudbysmith2024,stark2023alpha} and relate to the stratified tests of risk-limiting audits
\citep{spertus2022}. The certified zone follows risk control by exact tests \citep{bates2021}. Paired accuracy tests
on a shared sample \citep{foody2004} precede the comparison of two maps.

\citet{lehmann2026} report that geospatial foundation models grow overconfident under corruption, and that
confidence-based abstention cannot defer around their confidently wrong predictions. I applied no corruption. I tested
shifts of year and place instead: Ai2's fine-tuned AWF model, run through its v1-era pipeline (my re-run) at a random
sample near its area, and a preregistered blind test of my v1.2 fine-tune at fresh plots farther away.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:methods}

Every guarantee below is over the random draw of the labelled sample, for one sample read once unless stated. Labels
are taken as right unless the user states how often a reviewer errs.

\subsection{Windows, confidence and the review set}
\label{sec:ranking}

The window is the unit of ranking, comparison and estimation. Its decision is the majority class of its $4\times4$
pixels. For a pixel with logits $z_{(1)} \ge z_{(2)} \ge \dots$, the confidence is the margin
\begin{equation}
  m = z_{(1)} - z_{(2)}.
\end{equation}
For a binary logit map it is $|z|$, and for a published product it is the product's own confidence or top class
probability. A window's confidence is the mean over its pixels.

The review set at budget $b$ is the least confident $\max(1, \mathrm{round}(bN))$ of the $N$ valid windows. Each
flagged window carries the label-free cues it satisfies, such as a class boundary or a decision that changes under a
two-pixel crop shift. A review set says where to look first. It is not a sample, and labelling it does not give the
map's error rate.

A map can be read from different inputs in different places, such as radar alone under cloud. Given a raster of such
input conditions, the package treats each condition as its own population in every review set, estimate and
certified zone.

\subsection{Two maps without labels}
\label{sec:compare}

For two maps on one grid the package reports, without labels, the share of windows where their decisions differ and
how much each cue is enriched among those windows. A rate is read against the reseed floor, the rate at which
refitting the same head with another seed changes decisions. At one date a difference means at least one map is
wrong; across dates it may be change on the ground. Without labels the package never says which map is right. That
takes the sample of Section~\ref{sec:whichmap}.

\subsection{Error rate and per-class accuracy}
\label{sec:estimate}

Let $\theta$ be the share of the map's $N$ windows whose class differs from the reference class. A reviewer labels
$B$ windows drawn by a design fixed before any label.

\paragraph{Simple random sample.} The sampled error count is hypergeometric. The exact interval holds every error
count whose two tail probabilities both exceed 0.025. Its coverage is at least 95\% for every map and sample size.

\paragraph{The confidence design (default).} Five strata are cut at the quintiles of the margin, with weights
$W_h = N_h/N$, and the budget follows Neyman's allocation. With $p_h$ the error share among stratum $h$'s $n_h$
labels and $f_h = n_h/N_h$,
\begin{equation}
  \hat\theta = \sum_h W_h \, p_h, \qquad
  \hat v = \sum_h W_h^2 (1 - f_h) \, \frac{p_h (1 - p_h)}{n_h - 1}.
\end{equation}
The interval is Wilson's at the effective sample size $\hat\theta(1-\hat\theta)/\hat v$ \citep{korn1998}. It is
nominal: it has no finite-sample guarantee, and its coverage is known from simulation only. Beside it the package
prints the betting interval of Theorem~1 (Section~\ref{sec:agency}), which holds at every sample size. I call that
one the valid interval.

\paragraph{The class design.} A sample can instead be stratified by the map's own classes, with a floor of labels
per class \citep{olofsson2014}. Under that design or a random sample, each count behind a class's user's accuracy,
producer's accuracy and reference share has an exact hypergeometric interval at a level split by the union bound.
The class shares then hold together with probability at least 0.95, and so do the producer's accuracies.

\paragraph{Unjudged windows.} A reviewer may mark a window as impossible to judge. It is counted right at the
interval's lower end and wrong at its upper end. The interval then covers at least 95\% however the unjudged windows
were chosen.

\paragraph{A reviewer who errs.} Let $e_0$ be the share of correct windows the reviewer marks wrong and $e_1$ the
share of wrong windows the reviewer passes. The labels' error rate is $p = \theta(1 - e_1) + (1 - \theta) e_0$. If
the user states bounds $e_0 \le E_0 < 1$ and $e_1 \le E_1 < 1$, then
\begin{equation}
  \max\Bigl(0, \frac{p - E_0}{1 - E_0}\Bigr) \;\le\; \theta \;\le\; \min\Bigl(1, \frac{p}{1 - E_1}\Bigr),
\end{equation}
and both ends are attained. The package maps the interval's ends through these functions. The bounds, their
sharpness, the carrying over of coverage and the unjudged-window interval are proved in Lean~4 \citep{demoura2021}
with Mathlib \citep{mathlib2020}. The monotonicity of the package's own interval ends is checked numerically, not
proved.

\paragraph{A per-class audit of the confident calls.}
\label{sec:audit}
The audit fixes a confident zone from the map before any label, by default the most confident third of the windows,
and gives each predicted class's part of it an exact verdict against a stated bound $\alpha$. A small audit can name
a bad class but cannot clear a good one: at $\alpha = 0.05$ the verdict \textit{below} needs at least 72 error-free
labels.

\subsection{Certified zone}
\label{sec:zone}

A certified zone is a part of the map, fixed by confidence, whose error rate is at most $\alpha$ except on at most a
share $\delta$ of samples. The zone at coverage $c$ is the $n_c = \mathrm{round}(cN)$ most confident windows, and
$R(c)$ is the error rate inside it, for $c \in \{0.05, 0.10, \dots, 1\}$. Of a simple random sample of $B$ labels,
$b_c$ fall in the zone and $k_c$ of those are errors. The exact one-sided p-value against $R(c) > \alpha$ is
\begin{equation}
  p_c = P(X \le k_c), \qquad X \sim \mathrm{Hypergeom}\bigl(n_c,\ \lfloor \alpha n_c \rfloor + 1,\ b_c\bigr).
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Rules.} The prefix rule accepts coverages from the smallest upward while $p_c \le \delta$. It is
fixed-sequence testing \citep{angelopoulos2021}, valid on any map whatever the shape of $R(c)$. The Bonferroni rule
accepts every coverage with $p_c \le \delta/J$ over $J$ levels. A zone with no errors needs about
$\lceil \ln\delta / \ln(1-\alpha) \rceil$ labels, 45 at $\alpha = 0.05$ and the default $\delta = 0.1$, so the
package drops, before any label, the levels that expect fewer. A zone size can also be fixed before labelling
\citep{wiens2003,wiens2005}. With $L$ input conditions, each is certified inside itself at $\delta/L$.

\paragraph{What $\delta$ bounds.} $\delta$ bounds the probability that the statement is wrong over all draws,
counting those that certify nothing. Given that a zone was certified, the chance that it is wrong can exceed
$\delta$. $\alpha$, $\delta$ and the rule must be chosen before any label. An unjudged window counts as wrong. The
test takes no reviewer error rate, because a miss rate would have to hold inside the zone, where a model's confident
errors sit.

\paragraph{Sequential sample.} Rereading a growing sample runs a new test at each look. The sequential design keeps
the guarantee at every look. It labels a fixed random order of the windows from the top and tests each level by an
e-process, a mixture of the likelihood ratios of sampling without replacement \citep{robbins1970}. By Ville's
inequality \citep{ville1939} it ever reaches $1/\delta$ with probability at most $\delta$, whatever decides when
labelling stops. The closest published method \citep{xu2024} assumes the risk monotone in the
threshold and samples with replacement; here the risk need not be monotone. The price is 71 labels for a clean zone
at $\alpha = 0.05$ and $\delta = 0.1$, against 45 for one look.

\paragraph{A certificate that travels with the map.}
\label{sec:certificate}
A certified zone can be packed with its labelled sample and digests of the map. The sample's seed is a hash of the
map and of every setting that changes the result, so a verifier with its own copy of the map can rebuild the
certificate. The certificate takes on the issuer's word, and says so, that the labels are right and that the sample
was drawn once.

\subsection{Which map is more accurate}
\label{sec:whichmap}

Where two maps give a window the same class, both are right or both are wrong. Their accuracies therefore differ
only through the windows where they differ. Let $N$ be the windows both maps predict, $D$ those where they differ,
and $K_a$ and $K_b$ the differing windows where map $a$, and where map $b$, matches the truth. Then
\begin{equation}
  \Delta = \mathrm{acc}_a - \mathrm{acc}_b = \frac{K_a - K_b}{N}.
\end{equation}
So the labels go only to a simple random sample of $n$ of the $D$ differing windows. Each map's share of them gets
its exact interval at 97.5\%, and the two give an interval for $\Delta$ that holds with probability at least 95\%,
however the unjudged windows fall.

While $5n \le D$ the more accurate map is named by the exact two-sided sign test at 5\%, with every unjudged window
counted against the map being named. The sign test assumes independent draws and these are drawn without
replacement, so I proved two statements (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}). \textbf{Lemma~W}: among the sampled windows where
exactly one map is right, the count right in the more accurate map has a lower tail below one half no heavier than
a fair binomial's. \textbf{Corollary~W}: the test names the less accurate map at most 2.5\% of the time, for every
population, sample size and placing of the unjudged windows \claim{which-map-sign-test-wrong-map-rate}. For larger
samples the map is named when the interval excludes 0.

The verdict names the more accurate of these two maps on this area and date. It gives neither map's accuracy, and it
is not a verdict on the models that made them.

\subsection{An agency's stratified sample}
\label{sec:agency}

Agencies publish map accuracy from a stratified sample of reference points, with a normal-theory interval
\citep{stehman2014,olofsson2014} whose stated level is nominal. From the same points the package computes the same
numbers, each with the valid interval: overall accuracy, each class's user's accuracy, producer's accuracy and
reference share, and the difference of two maps' accuracies.

\paragraph{Setting.} The population is split into strata with shares $W_h$ and sizes $N_h$. Each unit carries a value
$x \in [0,1]$ with mean $\mu_h$ in stratum $h$, and the target is $\theta = \sum_h W_h \mu_h$. The $n_h$ labels of
stratum $h$ are a simple random sample without replacement. The strata are drawn independently, and each $n_h$ is
fixed before sampling.

\paragraph{Construction.} Each stratum's evidence is an e-value: a nonnegative statistic whose mean is at most 1 when
the tested mean is the true one. Mine is a betting product over the stratum's labels in draw order. At step $t$ it
bets
\begin{equation}
  \lambda_t = c\, W_h\, \frac{N_h - t}{N_h\, n_h}
  \label{eq:bet}
\end{equation}
on the next value against a running guess, with one bet size $c$ shared by all strata. I chose this bet so that the
logarithm of every stratum's e-value is linear in its mean with slope $-c W_h$. The product over strata then depends
on the stratum means only through $\theta$, so no split of $\theta$ among the strata needs to be searched. The
e-value is averaged exactly over the orders of each stratum's labels, then over a grid of bet sizes fixed before the
labels are read. Call the result $M(\theta_0)$ for a tested value $\theta_0$.

\textbf{Theorem 1 (the betting interval).} $M(\theta_0)$ decreases in $\theta_0$. Let $L$ be the largest
$\theta_0 \in [0, \sum_h W_h]$ with $M(\theta_0) \ge 2/\alpha$, or 0 if there is none, and let $U$ be the same
construction applied to the values $1 - x$, subtracted from $\sum_h W_h$. Then $P(L \le \theta \le U) \ge 1 - \alpha$
for every population and every allocation $(n_h)$ fixed before sampling.

Appendix~\ref{app:proofs} gives the full construction, the proof, and the extensions to strata given as areas and to
ratios such as the user's and producer's accuracy. Each interval is a 95\% statement on its own; the intervals do
not hold jointly. A point with no usable value is never dropped. It takes its least possible value for the lower end
and its greatest for the upper end.

\paragraph{What may be new.} The e-values follow the betting martingales of \citet{waudbysmith2020,waudbysmith2024}
and \citet{stark2023alpha}. Stratified audits search over the ways $\theta_0$ can be split among the strata
\citep{ottoboni2018,spertus2022,spertus2024}. What I did not find in that work is the bet of Eq.~\eqref{eq:bet},
which makes the search disappear. I also found no earlier use of a betting interval in the accuracy assessment of
maps.

\subsection{Evaluation protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

\paragraph{Units and statistics.} The graded unit is the window on segmentation maps and the sample on classification
tasks. The selective risk at a coverage is the error rate among the most confident units kept. AURC is its mean over
all coverages, and the excess AURC is AURC minus that of the perfect ranking \citep{geifman2019}. The failure AUROC,
written AUROC below, is the probability that the signal ranks a random error above a random correct unit. Where the
reference is a probability sample, statistics are weighted by its design \citep{olofsson2014}.

\paragraph{No-model controls.} A candidate signal is supported only if it beats both the model's confidence and a
control that does not use the model's scores: pixel statistics on imagery, and on released embeddings the distance
from the training mean and the rarity of the predicted class.

\paragraph{Kinds of reference.} A reference is not the truth, and its origin limits what a test can show. Only a
probability sample supports a statement about a whole map. On the OlmoEarth paper's embedding suite
\citep{herzog2025} the linear probes are my reruns under one recipe, scored by accuracy, so they are not comparable
with any paper's mIoU or F1.

\subsection{Confidence as the review signal}
\label{sec:res-confidence}

The model's own confidence orders a review well on benchmark splits, on published products and on references labelled
outside this project. It fails where the model is confidently wrong: under a shift of year and place, on an input
combination the classifier was not trained on, and on a legend boundary. It is not a probability to threshold.

\paragraph{The embedding suite.}
\label{sec:res-suite}
On the OlmoEarth paper's embedding suite, 24 of the 25 tasks define a margin. With linear probes on OlmoEarth Base's
frozen embeddings, the margin closes a median 0.68 of the gap between a random and a perfect review order (read after
the run, not preregistered) \claim{margin-takes-two-thirds-of-the-ranking-headroom}. As preregistered, it beats the
better of two controls that use no model on all 24 tasks \claim{suite-margin-wins-every-task}. Those controls are
weak: they close a median 0.094 of the gap and do no better than a random order on 9 of the 24 tasks, so the win
mostly shows that the margin beats chance (read after a red-team review, not preregistered)
\claim{suite-controls-are-near-chance}.

The finding replicates across sixteen encoders, OlmoEarth and eight other families. The margin beats a random order
in every one of 3,560 combinations of encoder, task and probe seed; the lowest AUROC is 0.503, for Clay Large on
Nandi Sentinel-1, close to chance (read after the runs, not preregistered)
\claim{exp79-margin-beats-random-everywhere}.

The margin is not new and I did not improve on it. One minus the top probability, the literature's baseline
\citep{hendrycks2017}, ranks errors slightly better on 14 of 16 multi-class tasks, by a median 0.0006 of excess AURC
\claim{top1-beats-the-margin-on-multiclass}. The margin beats the nearest-neighbour \citep{sun2022} and Mahalanobis
\citep{lee2018} distances on 24 of 24 tasks and a five-seed ensemble \citep{lakshminarayanan2017} on 22
\claim{suite-margin-beats-the-strong-alternatives}. No signal I derived from the encoder's internals, its pretraining
objective or a second view ranks errors better than confidence on expert labels
\claim{no-encoder-internal-signal-beats-confidence} (Appendix~\ref{sec:neg}).

\paragraph{References labelled outside this project.}
The preregistered leads held on three such references. On Dynamic World's 409 expert-annotated tiles, the margin of
the product's published probabilities gives an excess AURC of 0.0637, against 0.1120 for the control
\claim{dw-margin-ranks-a-production-model}. The published top probability understates the product's accuracy by
about 0.20 (0.539 against 0.738), so the same numbers order a review well and threshold badly
\claim{dw-published-probabilities-are-underconfident}. The lead also held on LUCAS, where a surveyor observed the
point \claim{lucas-ranking-survives-ground-observation}, and on EuroCrops \claim{eurocrops-ranking-holds-on-declarations}.

\paragraph{Ai2's fine-tuned AWF model, and the transfer miss.}
\label{sec:res-deployed}
The opening fact of Section~\ref{sec:intro} means that a stated accuracy needs a coverage
\claim{accuracy-needs-coverage}.

I then ran the model through its v1-era pipeline (my re-run; neither map below is one Ai2 published). At the 259 of
Ai2's validation points that fall on the re-run's map (33 errors), its confidence ranks the errors with AUROC 0.849,
and the least confident 10\% of the points hold 42.4\% of them; these preregistered predictions held
\claim{exp98-deployment-gives-the-evaluations-classes-and-ranking}. Ai2's points were placed by experts, not drawn at
random, so they give no error rate for the map.

The one probability sample near the AWF area is the East Africa TimeSync plots, a simple random sample within each
country, on 2017 imagery. Before any map I fixed a one-to-one crosswalk from their legend to AWF's, and a lenient one
that also accepts a named neighbouring class. Of the 309 plots within 100\,km, the 271 with 2017 input were graded;
the 38 without are not missing at random, so every figure describes the plots with input. Under the one-to-one
crosswalk the design-weighted AUROC of the confidence is 0.638 (90\% bootstrap 0.579 to 0.696), below my
preregistered bar of 0.70, so the prediction failed. The lenient crosswalk gives 0.678, with an interval, 0.599 to
0.756, that contains the bar \claim{exp99-confidence-misses-the-ranking-bar-under-both-rules}.

My own fine-tune of the task on OlmoEarth v1.2 Base (Ai2's recipe and labels, one training run) repeated the miss
under the same frozen rules: 0.642 under the one-to-one crosswalk (0.582 to 0.703), below 0.70 though the interval
reaches it, and 0.730 under the lenient one, with an interval that contains the bar
\claim{exp103-v12-transfer-ranking-misses-under-strict}. The two models share task, labels, region and recipe, so
this is a replication across model versions, not an independent test.

Most of the confident errors lie on one legend boundary. Of the confident errors under the one-to-one crosswalk, 14
of v1's 20 and 20 of v1.2's 22 are wrong only under that crosswalk, and 11 and 19 of these are one pair:
shrubland/savanna where TimeSync says Open Grassland (report-only, not preregistered, on plots already examined)
\claim{exp109-transfer-confident-errors-lie-on-one-contested-pair}. That boundary is a question for the model and its
legend, not for a verification layer.

\paragraph{A blind test at fresh plots.}
\label{sec:res-blind}
An earlier test missed my preset bar of 0.70. I preregistered the prediction that this miss would repeat at 890 fresh
plots; for my v1.2 fine-tune it did not: 0.729, though the lower end of its 90\% interval sits at 0.70. The plots are
a stratified draw of 900 Kenya and Tanzania TimeSync 2017 plots more than 100\,km from the AWF area, 890 with input,
none graded before. The AUROC is design-weighted back to the region, under the one-to-one crosswalk, with a 90\%
bootstrap interval of 0.701 to 0.758, so the run does not show it above the bar either
\claim{exp111-v12-ranking-reaches-the-bar-at-fresh-plots}. Ai2's v1 model failed a known-answer gate, so by an
amendment made after the gate and before any blind prediction I graded v1.2 and report v1 beside it
\claim{exp111-v1-does-not-reproduce-exp98-under-rslearn-0117}.

Three more predictions of this test failed. The lenient crosswalk did not rank the errors better. The errors that
exist only under the one-to-one crosswalk ranked at 0.656, against a predicted at most 0.65
\claim{exp111-v12-strict-only-errors-rank-just-above-the-bar}, and a 20-label per-class audit would flag the
grass/shrub pair with probability 0.287, not more often than not
\claim{exp111-v12-twenty-label-audit-does-not-reliably-flag-the-pair}. Why the two tests differ is not established
(Section~\ref{sec:limits}).

\paragraph{Ai2's Forest Loss Driver model.}
\label{sec:res-forestloss}
I ran Ai2's Forest Loss Driver model with Ai2's configuration on alerts published after it was trained: a random 300
of the 3,565 agriculture alerts and all 389 mining alerts that MapBiomas Alerta published for the Amazon biome from
January to June 2026, with the analysts' drivers taken as right. The model disagrees with them at 71 of the 689
alerts, 10.9\% design-weighted (exact interval 8.0\% to 14.4\%) \claim{exp104-forest-loss-agreement-with-mapbiomas}.
Its confidence ranks the disagreements at a design-weighted AUROC of 0.770, meeting the preregistered 0.70 on its
point value; the 90\% interval, 0.682 to 0.855, contains the bar
\claim{exp104-forest-loss-confidence-ranks-the-disagreements}.

\paragraph{A missing input.}
\label{sec:res-conditions}
A crop-type probe trained on Sentinel-1 plus Sentinel-2 embeddings of PASTIS, read without refitting on the
Sentinel-1 embeddings alone, errs on 73.6\% of 458,638 windows, against 19.5\% with full input. Of its errors, 59.8\%
are as confident as the median correct window under full input, against 6.0\%
\claim{missing-optical-errors-are-confident}. The cause is the mismatch, not the missing sensor (read after the run,
not preregistered): a probe trained on Sentinel-1 alone errs on 28.4\%, and its margin ranks the errors at AUROC
0.79, against 0.59 for the mismatched probe \claim{exp88-matched-head-ranks-normally}. Pooling two input conditions
hides the worse one. On a map with half its tiles read without the optical input, that half errs on 74.1\% of
windows and the rest on 19.7\%, and the interval of 300 random windows covers the worse half's rate on none of 2,000
draws \claim{pooled-error-rate-misstates-the-cloudy-part}. The package therefore reports each condition separately.

\paragraph{Another organisation's field map.}
\label{sec:res-ftw}
The confidence of FTW's deployed 2nd-edition global field map, graded on the FTW benchmark's test chips with the
benchmark's masks taken as right, ranks its window errors at AUROC 0.837, 0.870, 0.936 and 0.863 in Belgium, Croatia,
Slovenia and Sweden. It leads the best no-model control by the preregistered margin in three countries; in Belgium
the lead is 0.018 (90\% chip bootstrap 0.004 to 0.034). The prediction held in 3 of 4 countries
\claim{exp110-ftw-map-confidence-ranks-its-window-errors}.

\paragraph{Maps from OlmoEarth v1.3 embeddings on held-out territories.}
\label{sec:res-planteur}
On Ai2's PLANTEUR crop-type benchmark I fitted a logistic regression on v1.3 embeddings of four territories and
applied it to the fifth. The maps' confidence ranks their errors at AUROC 0.874, 0.857, 0.816, 0.876 and 0.742 in the
five held-out territories, meeting the preregistered bars in 5 of 5
\claim{exp113-v13-ranking-holds-on-held-out-territories}. But in three territories the held-out map is close to
calling everything Background \claim{exp113-held-out-maps-mostly-background}. A label-free estimate of the class
shares was closer to the truth than the map's own in 3 of 15 held-out maps, against a predicted at least 10
\claim{exp114-label-free-priors-mislead-under-feature-shift}. A good ranking does not make a usable map.

\subsection{Error rate from labels, and published products}
\label{sec:res-estimate}


The exact interval covers. On sixteen encoders and seven segmentation tasks, 111 cells with every window labelled,
the exact interval of 300 random windows has an exact coverage of at least 0.95
\claim{exp79-estimation-findings-hold-on-every-encoder}. Labels taken by tile and treated as independent do not
cover: on six of seven tasks the interval held the truth on 0.506 to 0.777 of draws
\claim{tile-sampling-breaks-the-naive-interval}.

The confidence design \citep{kumar2016} narrows the interval to a median 0.80 of a random sample's width, with
coverage intact \claim{confidence-helps-most-where-the-map-is-good}. Its interval is nominal. The valid interval
covered on at least 0.9225 of draws in each of 1,655 cells on 269 fully labelled maps, against a preregistered bar
of 0.917 \claim{exp120-valid-interval-covers-on-real-maps}.

Per class, the class design's exact intervals held as preregistered. On 269 fully labelled maps at 400 labels, the
user's-accuracy interval covered on at least 0.917 of draws for 2,401 of 2,402 (map, class) pairs; the one below, at
0.9125, has an exact coverage of 0.954 \claim{exp116-class-design-exact-intervals-hold}. The field's Wald interval
left 107 of 522 cells below 0.93 coverage, the worst at 0.019
\claim{per-class-intervals-wald-fails-wilson-nearly-holds}.

\paragraph{Published products.}\label{sec:res-products}
On two published land-cover products graded against an independent random sample, all four preregistered predictions
held. The reference is LCMAP's Reference Data Product \citep{pengra2020}, a simple random sample of the 30\,m pixels
of the conterminous United States; 24,968 of its plots stand in for the map. LCMAP Collection 1.3 is 82.3\% and
Esri's 10\,m land cover 79.6\% accurate against it, and they differ on 21.6\% of plots. Labels drawn among the
differing plots covered the 2.7-point difference on 98.5\% to 99.0\% of 2,000 draws, at about half the width of
labels drawn from all plots. Three hundred random plots gave an exact error-rate interval that covered on 95.0\% and
95.65\% of draws \claim{exp92-products-labelled-routes-hold}. LCMAP's own confidence ranked its errors at AUROC 0.753
(95\% bootstrap 0.734 to 0.771) \claim{exp92-lcmap-confidence-ranks-its-errors}. Esri is graded through a crosswalk,
so the difference measures agreement with this reference, not which product is better.

\subsection{Certified zone}
\label{sec:zone-results}

Used as specified, on one sample read once, the certified zone kept its guarantee on every map graded, and at a few
hundred labels it often certified nothing. On OlmoEarth Base's 24 tasks at 100, 300 and 1,000 random labels with
$\delta = 0.1$, the prefix zone exceeded $\alpha$ on at most 0.080 of 2,000 draws and the Bonferroni zone on at most
0.0045, on all 112 cells (110 with a testable level), against a bound of 0.120. The plug-in zone, the largest zone
whose sample error rate is at most $\alpha$, exceeded $\alpha$ on up to 0.557 of draws
\claim{trust-zone-guarantee-holds}. On three published products' confidences, at 300 labels and $\alpha$ half the
error rate, the prefix zone exceeded $\alpha$ on at most 2.8\% of draws and the plug-in zone on 39\% to 53\%, but
the prefix rule returned no zone on 73\% to 97\% of draws \claim{exp94-zone-guarantee-holds-on-three-products}.

The guarantee has two limits. It is per budget, not per look: certifying at every budget of a doubling ladder gave a
wrong zone at some look on 21\% to 30\% of draws \claim{certify-guarantee-is-per-budget-not-per-look}. The sequential
design kept the guarantee at every look, as preregistered: on 366 maps its largest violation rate was 0.1175 at
$\delta = 0.1$ (bound 0.175) \claim{exp97-sequential-certificate-holds-at-every-look}. And $\delta$ is not the chance
that an issued certificate is wrong: on 46 of the 413 cells where a rule with its zone fixed in advance certified on
some draw, more than $\delta$ of the zones it certified were wrong (descriptive, not preregistered)
\claim{exp116-issued-certificates-can-fail-more-often-than-delta}.

\subsection{Which map is more accurate}
\label{sec:res-whichmap}

\paragraph{Without labels.}
\label{sec:res-compare}
Without labels the package says where two maps differ, not which is right. Believing the side with the larger margin
is right on 51\% to 70\% of differing windows, below the better side wherever one is known
\claim{tool-vs-diff-resolution}.

\paragraph{With few labels.}
Labels drawn where two maps differ name the more accurate map far more often than the same number drawn from the
whole map. In a check that was not preregistered, on 2,514 pairs of my linear probes with the full truth, 100 labels
among the differing windows named the right map on 60.3\% of draws, against 12.7\% for 100 labels from the whole map.
On the pairs that differ on more windows than the budget, the rates were 42.8\% against 11.3\%. No pair named the
wrong map on more than 4.0\% of draws at that budget \claim{exp90-which-map-few-labels}. These are probes on frozen
embeddings, not map products; on published products the design kept its coverage (Section~\ref{sec:res-products}).

Against common practice, McNemar's test on one shared sample \citep{foody2004}, the design fell short of its
preregistered prediction. With exact probabilities on nine pairs of published products at 100 labels, it named the
better-agreeing map at least as often as the exact test on 8 pairs but as often as the uncorrected z form on only 6;
the prediction was 7 against both. The bars were set with the pairs' differences known
\claim{exp107-tool-design-against-mcnemar}.

\subsection{Agency samples}
\label{sec:res-agency}

Run end to end, the package reproduced the published overall accuracies of two agencies' stratified samples (not
preregistered): NLCD 2016's assessment \citep{wickham2021}, 4,629 points in 21 strata, and GFC2020's validation set
\citep{colditz2025,bourgoin2026}, 21,612 points in 149 strata. It gave 86.39\% for NLCD 2016 by the
primary-or-alternate rule, published as 86.4\%, and three more published figures to within 0.02 points; a fifth is
90.70\% against 90.6\%. GFC2020 is 91.51\%, published as 91\%
\claim{exp117-grade-reproduces-published-overall-accuracies}.

The valid interval costs width. Over all 10 overall accuracies and differences it was 1.60 to 1.64 times as wide as
normal theory's
\claim{exp117-valid-interval-costs-1-6-times-normal}. NLCD leads LCMAP by 1.34 points: 0.41 to 2.26 by normal theory,
which calls the lead significant, and $-0.17$ to 2.86 by the valid interval, which does not
\claim{exp117-normal-theory-calls-a-difference-the-valid-interval-does-not}.

The width buys coverage per class. In preregistered simulations at NLCD 2016's design (5,000 draws without
replacement from ten copies of each stratum's points), the valid intervals covered at least the bar of 0.9408 on all
91 quantities, so they fell short on none \claim{exp118-valid-intervals-hold-at-nlcd-shape}. Normal theory fell short
on 33 of 91, all per class: 16 of 29 producer's accuracies (least 0.835), 9 of 29 reference shares and 8 of 29 user's
accuracies (least 0.712). On the overall accuracies and the difference it covered 0.948 to 0.951
\claim{exp118-normal-theory-short-on-classes-not-overall}. One caveat: the interval method was chosen after its
widths were seen \claim{exp117-design-study-widths}. GFC2020's coverage was not measured.

\subsection{Use by a language-model agent}
\label{sec:res-agent}

With the package as tools, a 27-billion-parameter model reproduced the package's review set and grounded 99.4\% of
its numbers in tool outputs \claim{agent-benchmark-tool-arm-reproduces-the-package}, but a plain numpy sandbox
captured 0.904 of the package's errors \claim{agent-benchmark-sandbox-rediscovers-the-ranking} and grounded 94.2\%
\claim{agent-benchmark-grounding-not-decisive}, so two of the three preregistered predictions failed. At 7 billion
parameters my OlmoEarth Agent (a beta project of my own, built on Ai2's OlmoEarth Studio API and not an Ai2 product)
called the review-set tool on only 17 of 40 runs, so its preregistered prediction failed too
\claim{agent-benchmark-7b-agent-does-not-find-the-tool}.

Other failed predictions are only in Table~\ref{tab:failed} of Appendix~\ref{app:failed}. Among them: perturbing the input did not find the errors a model makes

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The labels are assumed right.} Every interval and certified zone describes agreement with the
  reviewer's labels. The reviewer-error bounds rest on rates the user states, and the certified zone has no allowance
  for reviewer error. I measured no human reviewer's error rate. The one reviewer I measured, a vision-language
  model, missed most of the map errors it judged.
\item \textbf{Most graded maps are linear probes on benchmark splits.} Apart from the published products and the
  fine-tuned models, the labelled evidence is linear probes on frozen embeddings, graded on benchmark splits that are
  not probability samples. The valid interval, the class design and the certificate are graded on these probes and
  one Dynamic World tile.
\item \textbf{Few published products and agency samples.} Seven products are graded against six reference samples,
  five of them probability samples. Only NLCD 2016's and GFC2020's samples are graded by their agencies' designs.
  The products are compared through crosswalks, and I did not check whether a reference point trained a product.
\item \textbf{Transfer is tested on one task.} The fine-tuned evidence is two models of one task, AWF, and Ai2's
  Forest Loss Driver model, graded in two of its ten classes. My replica of Ai2's AWF evaluation is graded on the 344
  validation points the checkpoint was chosen on \claim{fine-tuned-model-audit}. No run separates the year, the
  place, the legend boundary and the kind of point, so why the blind test and the earlier tests differ is not
  established.
\item \textbf{No corruption and no real cloud.} The cloudy condition I tested is whole tiles read without the
  optical input, on three crop families. The ranking under corruption of the kind \citet{lehmann2026} apply is not
  run.
\item \textbf{The ranking carries no guarantee.} A review set is not a sample. No label-free reading I tried
  identified the errors a model makes confidently.
\item \textbf{Some intervals are nominal.} The default confidence design's interval, and the per-class intervals
  under it, have no finite-sample guarantee. The tile design under-covers where a few tiles hold most of the errors.
\item \textbf{GPU type.} Probe training was not bit-reproducible across GPU types.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Proofs for the comparison of two maps}
\label{app:proofs-whichmap}

Two maps $a$ and $b$ differ at $D$ windows. $K_a$ and $K_b$ are the differing windows where map $a$, and where map
$b$, matches the truth. A simple random sample of $n$ of the $D$ windows is labelled. $r_a$ and $r_b$ are the numbers
of sampled windows where the reviewer finds map $a$ and map $b$ right, and $u$ the number marked \code{?}. Map $a$
is named only if the exact two-sided sign test at level $\alpha$ on $(r_a, r_b + u)$ names it, and map $b$ only if
the test on $(r_a + u, r_b)$ does.

\textbf{Lemma W.} Let $T = K_a + K_b \ge 1$ with $K_a \ge K_b$, and let $1 \le m \le T$. Draw a simple random
sample of $m$ of $T$ units, $K_a$ of which are marked, and let $X$ be the number of marked units in it, so that
$X \sim \mathrm{Hypergeom}(T, K_a, m)$. Then, for every integer $k < m/2$,
\begin{equation}
  P(X \le k) \le P\bigl(\mathrm{Bin}(m, 1/2) \le k\bigr).
  \label{eq:proof-lemma-w}
\end{equation}

\textit{Proof.} For $k < 0$ both sides are 0. First let $T = 2K$ and $K_a = K_b = K$. Write $f$ and $g$ for the
probability functions of $X$ and of $\mathrm{Bin}(m, 1/2)$ on $0, \dots, m$, and $r = f/g$ ($g > 0$ there). For $x$
and $x + 1$ both in the support of $f$,
\begin{align*}
  \frac{r(x+1)}{r(x)}
  &= \frac{f(x+1)/f(x)}{g(x+1)/g(x)}
   = \frac{(K - x)(m - x)}{(x + 1)(K - m + x + 1)} \cdot \frac{x + 1}{m - x} \\
  &= \frac{K - x}{K - m + x + 1},
\end{align*}
which is at least 1 exactly when $x \le (m-1)/2$. The support, $\max(0, m - K) \le x \le \min(m, K)$, is symmetric
about $m/2$, and so are $f$ and $g$. Hence $r(x) = r(m - x)$, and $r$, which is 0 off the support, does not increase
in $|x - m/2|$.

Fix $0 \le k < m/2$. Let $\mathcal{L} = \{x \le k\}$, $\mathcal{U} = \{x \ge m - k\}$ and $\mathcal{I}$ the integers
in between. By symmetry $f(\mathcal{L}) = f(\mathcal{U})$ and $g(\mathcal{L}) = g(\mathcal{U})$, and every value of
$r$ on $\mathcal{I}$ is at least every value of $r$ on $\mathcal{L} \cup \mathcal{U}$. If $\mathcal{I}$ is empty, $m$
is odd, $k = (m-1)/2$ and $f(\mathcal{L}) = 1/2 = g(\mathcal{L})$. Otherwise suppose
$f(\mathcal{L}) > g(\mathcal{L})$. Then $f(\mathcal{L} \cup \mathcal{U}) > g(\mathcal{L} \cup \mathcal{U})$, so
$r > 1$ somewhere on $\mathcal{L} \cup \mathcal{U}$, hence $r > 1$ everywhere on $\mathcal{I}$ and
$f(\mathcal{I}) > g(\mathcal{I})$. Then
\[
  1 = 2f(\mathcal{L}) + f(\mathcal{I}) > 2g(\mathcal{L}) + g(\mathcal{I}) = 1,
\]
a contradiction. So $f(\mathcal{L}) \le g(\mathcal{L})$, which is (\ref{eq:proof-lemma-w}).

Now let $K_a > K_b$. Adding one unmarked unit to a population of $T$ makes the marked count stochastically smaller.
To see it, draw a simple random sample of $m$ of the $T$ units and, with probability $m/(T+1)$, replace a uniformly
chosen member by the new unit. An $m$-subset without the new unit then has probability
\[
  \Bigl(1 - \frac{m}{T+1}\Bigr) \Big/ \binom{T}{m} = 1 \Big/ \binom{T+1}{m},
\]
and one with it, reached from $T - m + 1$ subsets, has probability
\[
  (T - m + 1) \cdot \frac{m}{T+1} \cdot \frac{1}{m} \Big/ \binom{T}{m} = 1 \Big/ \binom{T+1}{m}.
\]
So the result is a simple random sample of $m$ of the $T + 1$ units, and its marked count is never larger. Adding
$K_a - K_b$ unmarked units one at a time gives $P(X \le k) \le P(X' \le k)$ with
$X' \sim \mathrm{Hypergeom}(2K_a, K_a, m)$. As $m \le T \le 2K_a$, the first part applies to $X'$. $\square$

\textbf{Corollary W.} Whatever $D$, $n$, the level $1 - \alpha$ and the windows marked \code{?}, the sign test's
verdict names map $b$ with probability at most $\alpha/2$ when $K_a \ge K_b$, and map $a$ with probability at most
$\alpha/2$ when $K_b \ge K_a$.

\textit{Proof.} Take $K_a \ge K_b$; the other case swaps the maps. Let $r_a^*$ and $r_b^*$ be the numbers of sampled
windows truly right in $a$ and in $b$, and $m^* = r_a^* + r_b^*$. Given $m^*$, those windows are a simple random
sample of the $T = K_a + K_b$ windows where exactly one map is right, so $r_a^*$ given $m^*$ is
$\mathrm{Hypergeom}(T, K_a, m^*)$. Let $k_m$ be the largest $k$ with $2P(\mathrm{Bin}(m, 1/2) \le k) < \alpha$, or
$-1$. The two-sided test on $(x, y)$ with $x < y$ names $b$ exactly when $x \le k_{x+y}$. The verdict names $b$ only
if $r_b > r_a + u$ and $r_a + u \le k_{r_a+r_b+u}$. As the labels are taken to be right, $r_a^* \le r_a + u$ and
$r_b^* \ge r_b$. The p-value $2P(\mathrm{Bin}(x + y, 1/2) \le x)$ does not increase when $x$ falls or $y$ rises,
since
\[
  P\bigl(\mathrm{Bin}(m+1, \tfrac12) \le x\bigr)
  = \tfrac{1}{2} P\bigl(\mathrm{Bin}(m, \tfrac12) \le x\bigr)
  + \tfrac{1}{2} P\bigl(\mathrm{Bin}(m, \tfrac12) \le x - 1\bigr).
\]
So then $r_b^* > r_a^*$ and $r_a^* \le k_{m^*}$. As $P(\mathrm{Bin}(m^*, 1/2) \le k_{m^*}) < \alpha/2 < 1/2$,
$k_{m^*} < m^*/2$, and by Lemma~W the conditional probability of that event is at most
$P(\mathrm{Bin}(m^*, 1/2) \le k_{m^*}) < \alpha/2$. For $m^* = 0$, outside the lemma, $k_0 = -1$ and the event is
empty, which also covers $T = 0$. Averaging over $m^*$ gives the bound. $\square$

\paragraph{The inequality in the literature.} The inequality is Uhlmann's \citep{uhlmann1966}. As reported in a
summary of R.~M.~Dudley's exposition of it, the paper proves $P(X \le k) \le P(\mathrm{Bin}(m, K_a/T) \le k)$ for
$k \le m K_a/T - 1$. With $K_a/T \ge 1/2$ this gives (\ref{eq:proof-lemma-w}) for $k \le m/2 - 1$, which covers every
critical value of the sign test. I read neither the paper nor the exposition for this report, so the proof above is
complete without them. Hoeffding's comparison of sampling with and without replacement \citep{hoeffding1963} is not
enough here: it orders expectations of convex functions, not the exact distribution function that the critical
values use.

\subsection{Proofs for an agency's stratified sample}
\label{sec:proofs-agency}

\paragraph{Setting.} The population is split into sampled strata $h$ with shares $W_h > 0$. Stratum $h$ holds $N_h$
units; a stratum whose size is an area is a point frame, $N_h = \infty$, whose draws are independent. Each unit
carries a value $x \in [0, 1]$, with mean $\mu_h$ in stratum $h$. The $n_h \ge 1$ labels of stratum $h$ are a simple
random sample without replacement. The strata are drawn independently, and each $n_h$ is fixed before sampling. The
quantity graded is $\theta = \sum_h W_h \mu_h$.

Take the labels of stratum $h$ in draw order $t = 0, \dots, n_h - 1$, with $X_t$ the value at step $t$ and $S_t$ the
sum of the first $t$ values. When the stratum's mean is $\mu$, the mean of the units not yet drawn is
$m_t(\mu) = (N_h \mu - S_t)/(N_h - t)$ ($m_t(\mu) = \mu$ for a point frame). A bet size $c > 0$ is shared by all
strata. The bet at step $t$ is $\lambda_t = c W_h (N_h - t)/(N_h n_h)$ ($c W_h/n_h$ for a point frame), and the guess
is $c_t = \min\bigl((1/2 + S_t)/(t+1),\ \kappa/\lambda_t\bigr)$ with $\kappa = 0.9$. The stratum's e-value is
\begin{equation}
  K_h(\mu) = \prod_{t=0}^{n_h - 1} \bigl(1 + \lambda_t (X_t - c_t)\bigr)\,
  \exp\bigl(\lambda_t (c_t - m_t(\mu))\bigr).
  \label{eq:proof-evalue}
\end{equation}

\textbf{Lemma 1 (validity in a stratum).} At the stratum's true mean, $\mathrm{E}\,K_h(\mu_h) \le 1$.

\textit{Proof.} As $X_t \ge 0$ and $\lambda_t c_t \le \kappa$, the bet $1 + \lambda_t (X_t - c_t)$ is at least
$1 - \kappa > 0$, so every factor is positive. Given the labels before step $t$, $X_t$ is a uniform draw from the
$N_h - t$ units not yet drawn, whose mean is $m_t(\mu_h)$ (for a point frame, an independent draw of mean $\mu_h$).
With $y = \lambda_t (m_t(\mu_h) - c_t)$, the factor's conditional mean is $(1 + y)e^{-y} \le 1$, since
$1 + y \le e^y$. A product of nonnegative factors, each with conditional mean at most 1 given the past, has mean at
most 1 (condition on the past step by step). $\square$

The inequality $1 + y \le e^y$ is the last step in the proof of Theorem~3.2 of \citet{waudbysmith2020} (arXiv
version, Appendix~B.3), taken after their use of the inequality of \citet{fan2015}. The factor in
(\ref{eq:proof-evalue}) is the intermediate quantity of that proof.

\textbf{Lemma 2 (linearity).} $\log K_h(\mu) = A_h - c W_h \mu$, where $A_h$ does not depend on $\mu$.

\textit{Proof.} $\mu$ enters (\ref{eq:proof-evalue}) only through $-\lambda_t m_t(\mu)$. Its coefficient of $\mu$ is
$-\lambda_t N_h / (N_h - t) = -c W_h / n_h$ at every step (for a point frame, $-\lambda_t = -c W_h / n_h$ directly),
and over the $n_h$ steps these add to $-c W_h$. The rest,
\[
  A_h = \sum_{t=0}^{n_h - 1} \Bigl[\log\bigl(1 + \lambda_t (X_t - c_t)\bigr) + \lambda_t c_t
  + \frac{\lambda_t S_t}{N_h - t}\Bigr],
\]
its last term absent for a point frame, is free of $\mu$ because $c_t$ is. $\square$

So $\prod_h K_h(\mu_h) = \exp(\sum_h A_h - c\theta)$ depends on the stratum means only through $\theta$: no split of
$\theta$ among the strata needs to be searched.

\textbf{Lemma 3 (orders and the mixture).} Let $\bar A_h(c)$ be the logarithm of the average of $e^{A_h}$ over all
orders of stratum $h$'s labels, and let $c_1, \dots, c_K$ be fixed before sampling. Then
\begin{equation}
  M(\theta_0) = \frac{1}{K} \sum_{k=1}^{K} \exp\Bigl(\sum_h \bar A_h(c_k) - c_k \theta_0\Bigr)
  \label{eq:proof-mixture}
\end{equation}
satisfies $\mathrm{E}\,M(\theta) \le 1$ at the true $\theta$.

\textit{Proof.} The sample has no order of its own; list it in a uniformly random order. The sequence is then
distributed as draws made one at a time without replacement (for a point frame, it is the exchangeable draws
themselves), so Lemma~1 applies to it. The average over its $n_h!$ orders is the conditional expectation of
$K_h(\mu_h)$ given the set of labelled units. As $-c W_h \mu_h$ is the same in every order, that average is
$\exp(\bar A_h(c) - c W_h \mu_h)$, whose mean is $\mathrm{E}\,K_h(\mu_h) \le 1$ by Lemma~1. The strata are drawn
independently, so the mean of the product over strata is the product of the means, at most 1. By Lemma~2 the product
is $\exp(\sum_h \bar A_h(c) - c\theta)$. An average with fixed weights of quantities with mean at most 1 has mean at
most 1. $\square$

\paragraph{The average over orders and the grid.} $A_h$ depends on an order only through the counts of each value
seen so far. So a dynamic programme over those counts sums every order exactly, with no order drawn at random. The
grid holds $K = 12$ values of $c$, geometric from $c_{\mathrm{lo}}$ to $10\,c_{\mathrm{lo}}$, with
$c_{\mathrm{lo}} = \sqrt{2 \ln(2/\alpha) / V}$ and $V = \sum_h W_h^2/(4 n_h)$. The value $c_{\mathrm{lo}}$ is the bet
that is best to second order when every stratum has the largest variance, $1/4$; the larger values suit strata of
smaller variance, as accuracies near 1 give. The grid is fixed by the weights, the sample sizes and $\alpha$, never
by the labels.

\textbf{Theorem 1 (the betting interval).} $M(\theta_0)$ decreases in $\theta_0$. Let $L$ be the largest
$\theta_0 \in [0, \sum_h W_h]$ with $M(\theta_0) \ge 2/\alpha$, or 0 if there is none. Let $U$ be the same
construction applied to the values $1 - x$, subtracted from $\sum_h W_h$. Then $P(L \le \theta \le U) \ge 1 - \alpha$
for every population and every allocation $(n_h)$ fixed before sampling.

\textit{Proof.} Every $c_k > 0$, so each term of (\ref{eq:proof-mixture}) decreases in $\theta_0$, and $M$ is
continuous. If $L > \theta$ then $M(\theta) \ge M(L) \ge 2/\alpha$, which by Markov's inequality and Lemma~3 has
probability at most $\alpha/2$. The values $1 - x$ have the weighted sum
$\sum_h W_h (1 - \mu_h) = \sum_h W_h - \theta$, so $U < \theta$ also has probability at most $\alpha/2$. A union
bound over the two ends completes the proof. $\square$

\textbf{Proposition 1 (an area read as points).} Two kinds of stratum are read as a point frame: one given as an
area whose units have a stated area, and one whose weights are printed too coarsely to give one whole number of
units. Suppose such a stratum was in fact drawn without replacement from $N_h \ge N_{\mathrm{lo}}$ units,
$N_{\mathrm{lo}}$ being the fewest its size allows and at least $n_h$. (A stated unit area that allows fewer than
$n_h$ units is refused; weights that allow fewer give $N_{\mathrm{lo}} = n_h$, as $n_h$ units were drawn.) Then
multiplying its e-value by
\[
  \exp\Bigl(-\frac{c W_h (n_h - 1)}{2 (N_{\mathrm{lo}} - n_h + 1)}\Bigr)
\]
keeps its mean at most 1.

\textit{Proof.} Let $\lambda = c W_h / n_h$, and let $m_t$ be the true mean of the units not yet drawn. At the
stratum's true mean $\mu$ the factor's conditional mean is
\[
  \bigl(1 + \lambda(m_t - c_t)\bigr) e^{\lambda(c_t - \mu)} \le e^{\lambda (m_t - \mu)}.
\]
As $\mu \le 1$ and $S_t \ge 0$, $m_t - \mu = (t\mu - S_t)/(N_h - t) \le t/(N_{\mathrm{lo}} - n_h + 1)$ for
$t \le n_h - 1$. Deflating step $t$ by $e^{-\lambda t/(N_{\mathrm{lo}} - n_h + 1)}$ therefore gives factors with
conditional mean at most 1, and
\[
  \sum_{t < n_h} \frac{\lambda t}{N_{\mathrm{lo}} - n_h + 1} = \frac{c W_h (n_h - 1)}{2 (N_{\mathrm{lo}} - n_h + 1)}.
\]
The deflation depends neither on the order nor on $\mu$, so Lemmas~2 and~3 hold as before. $\square$

\textbf{Proposition 2 (the box for ratios).} A user's or producer's accuracy is $R = \theta_y / (\theta_y + \theta_w)$
with indicators $y$ and $w$, $y + w \le 1$ at each unit. For the user's accuracy of class $j$, $y$ marks a unit
mapped $j$ and right, and $w$ one mapped $j$ and wrong. For the producer's, $y$ marks a unit mapped $j$ whose
reference class is $j$, and $w$ one of reference class $j$ mapped otherwise. Let $[L_y, U_y]$ and $[L_w, U_w]$ be
the betting intervals at $\alpha/2$, and let $\theta_y + \theta_w > 0$ (a class the map never gives has no user's
accuracy, one the reference never holds no producer's). Then
\[
  R \in \Bigl[\frac{L_y}{L_y + U_w},\ \frac{U_y}{U_y + L_w}\Bigr]
\]
with probability at least $1 - \alpha$, an end whose denominator is 0 being read as 0 below and 1 above.

\textit{Proof.} Both intervals hold together with probability at least $1 - \alpha$ by the union bound. $R$
increases in $\theta_y$ and decreases in $\theta_w$:
$\partial R/\partial\theta_y = \theta_w/(\theta_y + \theta_w)^2 \ge 0$ and
$\partial R/\partial\theta_w = -\theta_y/(\theta_y + \theta_w)^2 \le 0$. So on that event $R$ lies between its
values at those ends; an end read as 0 or 1 holds trivially. $\square$

The package also offers one betting test of $\theta_y - r(\theta_y + \theta_w) \le 0$ inverted over $r$ in place of
the box. Its monotonicity argument is not proved here, and the box stays the default.

\textbf{Proposition 3 (the pure-stratum rule).} An agency's design can hold, for a class $j$, a stratum $o$ every
unit of which the map labels $j$, such as NLCD's no-change strata. Let $\mathcal{M}$ be the strata declared pure for
no class (a stratum pure for another class holds no unit mapped $j$). Let $p_o$ be the share of $o$'s units that are
right, and $a_h$ and $b_h$ the shares of stratum $h$'s units mapped $j$ and right, and mapped $j$ and wrong. Then
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{UA}_j = \frac{W_o p_o + \sum_{h \in \mathcal{M}} W_h a_h}{W_o + \sum_{h \in \mathcal{M}} W_h (a_h + b_h)}.
  \label{eq:pure}
\end{equation}
Take the exact hypergeometric interval for a share from a simple random sample without replacement, each tail at
half the complement of its level. Use it for $p_o$ at level $1 - (\alpha - \gamma)$, and for each $a_h$ and $b_h$ at
$1 - \gamma/(2M')$. Here $M'$ is the number of strata of $\mathcal{M}$ not labelled in full (a stratum labelled in
full is known exactly), and $\gamma = \alpha/10$ ($\gamma = 0$ when $M' = 0$). With probability at least
$1 - \alpha$, $\mathrm{UA}_j$ lies between two values of (\ref{eq:pure}). The first is taken at the lower end of
$p_o$, the lower ends of the $a_h$ and the upper ends of the $b_h$, and the second at the opposite ends.

\textit{Proof.} By the union bound all the intervals hold together except with probability at most
$(\alpha - \gamma) + 2M' \gamma/(2M') = \alpha$. $\mathrm{UA}_j$ increases in $p_o$, which enters only the
numerator. It increases in each $a_h$, which adds $W_h a_h$ to numerator and denominator alike, the ratio being at
most 1 at every point of the box as $p_o \le 1$ and $b_h \ge 0$. It decreases in each $b_h$, which enters only the
denominator. So, moving one coordinate at a time, on that event $\mathrm{UA}_j$ lies between its values at those
ends. $\square$

The rule needs every stratum's size in whole units, every stratum sampled and no unknown value. It applies to the
user's accuracy of the map the strata were drawn from, for a class with exactly one declared pure stratum; a class
with two or more takes the box of Proposition~2. A declaration contradicted by a labelled point is refused; at the
units no one labelled, the declaration is the agency's statement.
